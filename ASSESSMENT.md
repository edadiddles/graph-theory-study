# Diagnostics, exercise selection, and milestone assessment

## Purpose and status

This assessment design and the [revised roadmap](ROADMAP.md) are adopted, with
conditional boundaries where source inventories remain pending. These instructions define how to
prepare and assess a milestone when its local scope is agreed; no diagnostic
questions, quiz questions, model answers, grades, or learner results are supplied
by preparing this document. Apply [AGENTS.md](AGENTS.md) throughout.

The learner prefers short, potentially irregular study sessions. Initial core
estimates target 4-6 active hours per milestone, including assessment. These
estimates are not time limits. Remediation and optional technology work receive
separate estimates. Split work at meaningful boundaries if the estimate or actual
experience indicates overload, retaining full reading/proof coverage.

## Prepare the milestone

Before study, the agent prepares a compact milestone card in the relevant
existing lab notes, identified by the revised M-ID. Finalize it with the learner
as part of local scope review. Include:

- Verified reading scope, including every assigned section and supplied proof.
- Required objectives, each with an ID and intended depth: explain, apply, or
  justify/prove. State the actual concept or claim, not merely a chapter title.
- Ancillary prerequisites and a short diagnostic plan, including what each
  check can reveal. These are prerequisite checks, not tests of untaught content.
- Agent-selected exercise purposes and verified references, adjusted after the
  diagnostic. Identify independent work and allowed supporting library calls.
- Estimated active time for diagnostic, reading/proofs, exercises, lab work,
  and mixed assessment; flag low-confidence estimates.
- Short-session stopping points and a quiz blueprint mapping required objectives
  to appropriate independent evidence. Keep actual assessment questions ungenerated
  until the learner is ready.

Do not force every objective to require a proof or independent algorithm
implementation. Match the declared depth to the actual learning goal. Reading
and reviewing every supplied proof remains a separate requirement even when
only selected proofs are used for independent reconstruction on a quiz.

## Diagnose and focus prerequisite work

Ask a small set of prerequisite questions without supplying their answers.
Use the learner's reasoning to distinguish a notation issue, forgotten fact,
conceptual gap, or missing method. A diagnostic is not a punitive grade and does
not establish mastery of the new milestone. Existing prior evidence can reduce
redundant checks, but do not assume familiarity from degree or job title alone.

For an identified gap, choose one focused explanation, section, lecture segment,
or practice task from owned references or [SUPPLEMENTS.md](SUPPLEMENTS.md). Record
why it fits, the expected evidence of readiness, and a separate effort estimate.
Recheck with a different small example before using the prerequisite in the main
work. Assign another resource only when the first does not address the problem.

Before random graphs, check conditional probability, independence, expectation,
variance, counting, elementary probability bounds, and asymptotic language as
needed by the assigned sections. Before second-moment arguments, check the
relevant moment/covariance reasoning. Before Chung, check symmetric spectral
decomposition, quadratic forms, Rayleigh/variational reasoning, weighted inner
products, and the finite-state chain concepts the upcoming unit uses. Introduce
additional inequality or analysis preparation only when the actual proof needs it.

After an interruption, use the session resume note and a short retrieval check
on relevant earlier prerequisites. Refresh the observed gaps before proceeding;
do not require an arbitrary number of days or a full-course restart.

## Select textbook exercises as the agent

Choose the set when the milestone is reached, using diagnostics, earlier attempts,
prior quiz outcomes, the learner's interests, and the declared objectives.
Exercise count is not a goal. A small set can cover multiple purposes:
definitions/hypotheses, a proof or counterexample, application, and transfer to
an unfamiliar example. Select more only when a meaningful gap remains uncovered.

Verify the edition and actual statement before assigning an identifier. Use
legitimate published previews or the learner's relevant exercise pages when
needed; access to a purchased physical book is not automatically available to
the agent. Record unverified choices as pending and resolve them before assignment.
Do not invent numbers or choose from an unauthorized solution repository.

Record each selected exercise's reference, purpose, objective IDs, permitted
aids, attempts, assistance, and review outcome in the lab notes. The learner's
role is to attempt and discuss the selected work, not to supply a generic
exercise list. Preserve attempts and explanations when adjusting the selection.

## Evidence levels and quiz blueprint

| Depth | Expected evidence | Insufficient evidence on its own |
|---|---|---|
| Explain | Precise meaning, assumptions, and interpretation in the learner's own words | Recognizing a term or recalling a library name |
| Apply | Appropriate use on an unfamiliar small example, with interpretation | Reproducing a memorized fixture or obtaining a plausible number |
| Justify/prove | The specified logical argument, invariant, certificate, or proof, with hypotheses and scope | Passing software checks or agreement with a library |

Before study, state which required objective needs which level and what kind
of evidence will assess it. The blueprint includes a small relevant retrieval
component from earlier work. Do not add unfamiliar requirements during grading.
If a new requirement is discovered, review and teach it before assessing it.

| Objective ID and statement | Required depth | Planned closed-book evidence | Planned open-book evidence | Readiness evidence and gaps |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| | | | | |

Keep question identifiers and actual responses in the assessment record when the
assessment happens. The blank table is a blueprint template, not a completed quiz.

## Administer the mixed quiz

Begin only after the required study and independent attempts are ready for review.
Generate fresh questions with verified expectations and the agreed scope. Avoid
repeating the exact worked exercise or test fixture as the only evidence of
transfer. Verify mathematical statements and tool conventions before grading;
an ambiguous or unverified item remains pending until resolved.

1. **Closed-book reasoning:** use no notes, textbook, code, internet search,
   answer-producing tool, or substantive agent help. Assess understanding,
   hypotheses, and reasoning at the agreed depth. Equivalent notation and valid
   alternate arguments are acceptable. Timing is for effort calibration only.
2. **Open-book application:** permit the declared notes, books, code, and library
   operations on a separate unfamiliar task. The learner selects and justifies
   the approach. Supporting tools can accelerate computation; they cannot bypass
   an independently assessed algorithm task or supply the reasoning being graded.

Questions can be delivered in manageable batches, with clear pause/resume points.
Clarify wording or access issues without revealing a method. During assessment,
do not provide leading questions, correction steps, or solutions. A request for
substantive help changes that attempt to assisted practice for the affected
objective; apply the existing gradual-help policy and assess it independently
later. A request for a full solution still requires the separate confirmation
specified in AGENTS.md and cannot count as independent assessment evidence.

## Decide readiness and remediate

Record a judgment and its supporting response for each required objective:

- **Ready:** demonstrated at the declared depth without substantive assistance.
- **Needs focused review:** the attempt reveals an identified gap or needed
  substantive help. Name the gap and the next practice task.
- **Pending:** evidence is missing, the task was interrupted, or the question or
  grading needs verification. Do not label uncertainty as a learner misconception.

An aggregate score may be descriptive, but it cannot compensate for an unmet
required objective. Advance only when all required objectives are ready and the
unit's reading/proof/exercise obligations are reviewed. No numerical pass mark
is imposed. Familiarity objectives are assessed at explain depth; they are not
silently converted into proof obligations.

Distinguish a minor slip from an underlying misconception using the reasoning
shown. Verify a correction without automatically repeating the whole quiz.
For a substantive gap, provide focused review and practice under gradual-help
rules, then use a fresh comparable independent task for that objective and its
necessary connections. Do not require repeating unrelated objectives already
supported by evidence. Repeated difficulty should prompt a different explanation,
another prerequisite check, or a reviewed split in scope rather than lowering
the standard silently or cycling through the same answer.

## Session, assessment, and completion records

Use existing `notes.md` files, separating M-IDs when a lab spans milestones.
Record objective IDs, quiz task references, permitted aids, original responses,
assistance, readiness evidence, review/practice assignments, reassessment, and
actual active-time ranges. A compact end-of-session note records where I stopped,
the next action, and an unresolved question. Preserve originals after corrections.

Each new milestone gets its own mixed assessment. The existing source lab's
final review checks that all of its assigned units and original tasks are covered;
it need not duplicate every quiz. Core unit-test outcomes, independent proof
evidence, supplied-proof review, and application interpretation remain distinct.
Preparing templates or receiving help never marks a learning requirement complete.
