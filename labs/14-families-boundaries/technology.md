construction, retaining family-level reasoning and boundary-model distinctions.

and selected small-construction/proof investigations remain learner work.

studied; defer that inspection when it would reveal the current exercise.

## Supporting operations

Graph containers, file interchange, and already-understood numerical solvers may
support experiments. Choose family parameters, comparison quantities, and modest
sizes before generation. Identify whether the chosen library object is simple,
directed, weighted, or permits parallel edges before applying the lab's formulas.
Do not silently collapse or symmetrize a generated object.

## Comparison after review

After the relevant small construction and reasoning review, use a matching
NetworkX generator for additional instances. Explain the definition and any
parameter restrictions even when the generator implementation is delegated.
After core discrepancy and boundary work is reviewed, supporting implementations
may accelerate finite comparisons. Neither a function name nor a few numerical
instances certify expansion, quasi-randomness, or a general construction theorem.

## Validation and report

Check the expected finite construction properties independently on small cases,
including vertex count, degree, direction, and multiplicity as applicable. Map
any projection explicitly before comparing spectra or cuts. Retain separate
full-graph degrees and induced-graph degrees for boundary experiments. Verify
operator ordering and zero-degree conventions. Record finite observations and
family-level mathematical guarantees separately, with actual theorem hypotheses.
